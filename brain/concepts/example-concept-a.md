## What it is

An *atomic note* captures a single idea in its own file, written in your own words, so it
can stand alone and be reused. Instead of long mixed documents, knowledge is broken into
small, self-contained units that you connect to each other.

## Why it matters

Small single-idea notes are easy to link, reuse across contexts, and update without
disturbing unrelated material. Atomicity is what makes a vault's link graph meaningful — a
note about one idea can be referenced precisely from many others.

## Key facts

- A note is "atomic" when it expresses one idea you could cite on its own. Source: example placeholder.
- Atomicity makes [[example-concept-b]] (linking notes together) far more useful — precise units link precisely. Source: example placeholder.
- Writing in your own words (not quoting) is what turns a captured source into durable knowledge. Source: example placeholder.

## How to apply it

- When a note starts covering two distinct ideas → split it into two.
- Title each note as a short claim or concept name, not a vague topic.
- Anti-pattern: dumping a whole article into one note — that defeats reuse.
